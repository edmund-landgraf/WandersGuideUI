Why the sites look the same What the functions do

Player view

On a campaign encounter you see the party in full and living enemies in short form.

Allies

Every player character and every ally creature shows name, initiative, defenses, hit points, and conditions. They stay on the grid at 0 HP.

Enemies

A living enemy shows initials, initiative, and conditions. Defenses and hit points are hidden, and the row has no level or side line. The full sheet stays closed. A creature with no stored hit points still counts as alive. At 0 HP or below, that enemy drops off your grid.

Say, for example, you have a combat already underway. The party is on the grid under their real names, with initiative, defenses, hit points, and conditions. Across from them the game master has put a Blue-Ringed Octopus, three Mudjaw Lurkblooms, a Reefclaw, and a Grindylow. You are not the game master, so you do not see those names written out. Your rows read BRO, ML (1), ML (2), ML (3), R1, and G1. Each row still has an initiative and any conditions the creature is carrying. It does not have a level, it does not say enemy, and it does not show armor class, saves, or hit points. You cannot open the sheet.

The short name is built from the name the game master stored. Each word contributes its first letter, and a hyphen splits a word the same way a space does, so Blue-Ringed Octopus is three letters: B, R, and O. A copy written as a number in parentheses keeps that number beside the initials, which is why the three lurkblooms are ML (1), ML (2), and ML (3) rather than three identical ML rows. A copy written as a number after the name sticks that number onto the initials with no space and no parentheses, which is why Reefclaw 1 is R1 and Grindylow 1 is G1.

Those labels are meant to be used with no other context. You do not need to remember the full creature name, you do not need the level the game master can see, and you do not need a line that says which side of the fight the row is on. If someone at the table says “ML (2) is frightened,” you look for the row whose name is ML (2) and whose conditions include frightened. If someone says “R1 goes next,” you look at the initiative on the R1 row. The words Mudjaw, Reefclaw, and octopus are useful color, but the grid does not require them. The initials are the name you share.

A creature that has just been added often has no hit points stored yet. On your grid that creature still counts as alive, so it stays in the list with the others. The game master’s grid treats that same empty hit-point value as full health. You still only see the initials.

Now say the fight moves. ML (1) is dropped to 0 hit points, and a little later ML (3) is dropped below 0. Both of those enemies leave your grid. They do not turn into a greyed-out row the way a player character does at 0 hit points. Allies and player characters stay visible at 0. Dead enemies do not. BRO, R1, and G1 are still there, and the lurkbloom that is still standing is still ML (2).

That last part is the reason the number is worth keeping. The grid does not renumber the survivors. ML (2) does not become ML (1) just because it is the only lurkbloom left, and it does not become a bare ML. If another reefclaw had been Reefclaw 2, and Reefclaw 1 died, the survivor would still be R2. You can keep a note, a condition call, or a sentence in the round log aimed at ML (2) after the rest of the list has changed, and it still points at the same creature. The game master, who still sees the full names, is looking at Mudjaw Lurkbloom (2). You are looking at ML (2). You are talking about the same row.

Sheets

You can edit your own character. Other player characters and ally creatures are read only. The game master can still edit those sheets.

The round log shows ally turns. A round that only had enemies is omitted.
